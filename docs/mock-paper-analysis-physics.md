## 2. What could not be analysed

These need the question papers, which are not available:

- How many questions each unit received in past years.
- Which concepts were asked most often.
- The real split of easy, medium and hard questions.
- The real share of each question format (assertion-reason, match the following, and so on).

So nothing in the blueprint below is a measured trend. Each figure is either an official rule or a stated choice of ours. If licensed papers are obtained later, section 4 can be replaced with measured weights.

The row count comes from how NTA's PDF lists the unit's bullets. It measures how much syllabus text a unit has, not how hard or how important it is. This is the weakest part of the blueprint and the first thing to replace when real distribution data exists.

Within a unit, no topic supplies more than two questions unless the unit's quota cannot be met otherwise. The trial paper built today drew on 29 of the 32 topics.

### 4.2 Difficulty

| Level | Share | Questions | In the bank |
|---|---|---|---|
| Easy | 30% | 15 | 185 |
| Medium | 50% | 25 | 405 |
| Hard | 20% | 10 | 197 |

A paper is refused if its mix is more than 10 percentage points from this target.

### 4.3 Formats

The bank holds 517 single-answer questions, 104 assertion-reason, 71 diagram-based, 53 statement-based and 42 match-the-following. The builder does not fix a format quota, because no official figure exists for one. A reviewer checks the spread when approving the paper.

### 4.4 Conceptual depth

Every question in the bank carries a skill tag written with it (for example recall, application, analysis) and an explanation of at least 132 characters. The builder does not yet balance by skill tag. A reviewer is asked to confirm, for each paper, that it is not mainly recall.

## 5. Capacity

A paper never repeats a question used in another mock paper. With the quotas above, the smallest pool per paper is Current Electricity, Dual Nature, and Atoms and Nuclei at 4 each. The bank's hard questions (197 for 10 per paper) and the smallest unit (62 questions for 4 or 5 per paper) allow **about 12 papers**, once the questions are approved.

## 6. Quality gates before a paper is published

1. Every question is approved by a reviewer on the reviewer screen (answer key, explanation, match to the syllabus topic).
2. The paper is built only from approved questions, to the quotas above.
3. A reviewer reads the paper as a whole and approves it with a written note on coverage, balance and keys.
4. A member of staff publishes it.
5. If any question in a published paper is later flagged, the paper disappears from students at once.
